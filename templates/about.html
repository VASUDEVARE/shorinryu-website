The Art

What is Karate?

Karate (空手) — meaning "Empty Hand" — is a martial art developed in the Ryukyu Kingdom (present-day Okinawa, Japan). It emphasises striking techniques using punches, kicks, and open-hand moves, combined with deep philosophical principles.

"The ultimate aim of karate lies not in victory or defeat, but in the perfection of the character of its participants." — Gichin Funakoshi

Kihon — Foundational techniques and stances practiced repeatedly for muscle memory

Kata — Pre-arranged sequences encoding traditional combat applications

Kumite — Sparring practice in dynamic, controlled combat scenarios

Kobudo — Traditional Okinawan weapons (bo, sai, nunchaku, tonfa, kama)

Our Style

Shorin Ryu Karate

Shorin-Ryu is one of the oldest and most traditional schools of Okinawan karate. The Matsubayashi-Ryu branch was founded in 1947 by O'Sensei Shoshin Nagamine, incorporating both Shuri-te and Tomari-te traditions.

Matsubayashi translates to "Pine Forest" — symbolising strength, flexibility, and endurance through all seasons.

18 Kata in the Matsubayashi-Ryu syllabus

Kobudo weapons training integrated with empty-hand study

Recognised by WKF for sports competition

Practiced in over 30 countries worldwide
